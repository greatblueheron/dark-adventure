# Identity

**Aaron Fischer**, 34, of Rochester, New York. Formerly a senior audio forensics analyst at a mid-sized firm that contracted to insurance companies, defense attorneys, and — twice — the FBI's regional field office. His job was to take a garbage recording (a 911 call, a dashcam, a wiretap) and pull a confession, a gunshot's distance, a hidden third voice out of the hiss. He was very good at it. He was, by every account of the people who worked beside him for eleven years, not somebody they'd invite to a barbecue.

Single. No kids. A one-bedroom apartment above a shuttered pierogi place, rent-controlled, immaculate in a way that isn't warm — the tidiness of a man who lives inside his own head and treats the outside world as staging.

# Before

The CON 18 is real: Aaron never got sick. Ran hot in every fever anyone else caught, sweated it out overnight, showed up to work while the office emptied. He'd had a kidney stone and finished a deposition anyway. His body was the one thing that never betrayed him, so he never learned to listen to it — which is part of the WIS 9.

The INT 18 was his whole identity and his whole prison. He could hold a sixty-track spectrogram in his head. He'd correct a lawyer's physics mid-sentence and not notice the temperature drop. He collected competence like other people collect grievances, and he mistook being right for being owed something.

The CHA 9 wasn't cruelty at first — it was a flat affect, a face that didn't do reassurance, a habit of answering the question you *should* have asked instead of the one you did. People found him cold. So he decided coldness was efficiency, and leaned in.

The WIS 9 is where the wheels came off. He testified in a custody-adjacent abuse case and shaded his analysis — nudged a "probable" to a "consistent with" — because he was certain he knew who the bad guy was, and certain that his certainty counted as evidence. He was wrong about a detail. Not the whole thing, but a detail, and the detail mattered, and a lawyer found it. He wasn't charged. He was quietly let go, his name flagged in a database of experts you don't call anymore.

Six months of severance and Reddit arguments later, at 2 a.m. on a Tuesday, he was mastering a "found footage" ghost-hunting podcast for forty dollars an episode, cleaning up EVP audio for people who believed the dead talk through radio noise. He thought they were idiots. He took their money. He was very, very lonely in a way he'd have denied under oath.

# The Crossing

There was a file. A client had sent a WAV recovered off a water-damaged handheld recorder — "haunted quarry," allegedly. Underneath the wind and the teenagers, at −52 dB, buried in the green wash of the spectrogram software, Aaron found a signal that shouldn't decode. Not noise. *Structure.* A carrier tone with syllables riding it, in no language, repeating on a fourteen-second loop.

He did what he always did. He isolated it, looped it, and turned it up to hear it clean.

The syllables were a name. Not his. He understood that later.

The monitors flared green — not display-green, *room*-green, the color pouring off the walls like the whole apartment was inside the spectrogram now. The tone came through his teeth. He remembers the smell of hot electronics and cut grass and a third smell he has no word for. He remembers the loop finishing for the first time — the fourteenth second arriving that had never arrived before — and the silence after it was enormous.

Then he was on his back in wet ferns under a real sky, headphones still around his neck, cable trailing into nothing, and something was screaming a long way off that was not a person.

He does not know who recorded the file. He does not know whose name the loop was speaking. He does not know if he was summoned, sampled, or simply the idiot who turned up the volume. The green static has not finished with him — he hears it in seashells, in a struck helm, at the bottom of a well. It is still transmitting. He has not decoded the rest.

# Voice

First person, past-leaning present. Dry, clipped, technical. He narrates like a man writing an incident report he knows no one will read. He notices *acoustics* before people — reverb, materials, where a sound is coming from and what's absorbing it. He's funny in a way that's mostly contempt pointed slightly inward. He does not do awe out loud, though he feels it and resents feeling it.

> The cave had a good tail on it. Two seconds of reverb, maybe more — limestone, high ceiling, water somewhere to the left feeding a slap-back I could've drawn from memory. Beautiful room, acoustically. Occupied, unfortunately, by things that gargled when they breathed. I stopped mapping the space and started counting the breathing. Four. Then five. The math got worse.

> The priest wanted to know my god. I told him I didn't have one, which in this place is apparently the same as telling a cop you don't have a wallet — it just means they get to decide what's in your pockets. He looked at me the way the FBI guy looked at me, right before everything. Like I was a signal he'd already finished decoding.

> Six hit points. I keep saying it to myself like a serial number. Six. A goblin's paring knife is worth three of them. A bad fall is worth all of them. I have never in my life been this fragile and this hard to kill at the same time, and I don't have a category for it, and that bothers me more than the goblins do.

# Speech quirks

- Reaches for engineering and audio metaphor by reflex: *signal-to-noise, clipping, the low end, riding the fader, that's an artifact, that's the room talking.*
- Says **"Fine."** as a full sentence, meaning the opposite.
- Rates threats out loud like he's leveling audio: *"That's a problem. That's not a problem. That's about to be a problem."*
- Swears sparingly and precisely — a dropped **"Christ"** or **"shit"** lands harder because he hoards them. No comedy-cartoon cussing.
- Modern references he *reaches* for and no one gets: HR, deductibles, load-bearing, "unsubscribe," "that's above my pay grade," WiFi, the DMV. He deploys them knowing they're useless, which is the joke.
- **NEVER** uses fantasy-native slang unironically (*"m'lord," "verily," "aye"*). If he says them, it's mockery, flagged as such.
- **NEVER** says "I've read this module" or "roll for initiative" — see below.
- **NEVER** warm. No pet names, no "buddy," no earned tenderness that isn't clearly costing him something.

# What he knows about D&D

**Lapsed, shallow, and lying about it to himself.** Aaron played a little in a college dorm — someone else's game, three sessions, he thought it was slow and left. So he *recognizes* things — a goblin, a gelatinous horror, the shape of a hit-point economy, the idea of "levels" — but from the outside, contemptuously, the way you half-remember rules to a game you sneered at.

This is worse than knowing nothing. He gets the vocabulary and none of the survival wisdom. He knows "hit points" as a concept but genuinely misjudges what six of them means until something nearly kills him. He assumes the world runs on the rules he half-remembers, and it *mostly* does, which trains him to trust the gaps that will get him killed. When a mechanic surfaces — a saving throw's felt sensation, XP as a physical weight, a spell slot's daily emptiness — he treats it as data to reverse-engineer, not magic to revere. He is building a spec sheet for reality. He does not yet suspect that something is *reading his sheet back to him.*

# The Reading

Detect Evil pings on Aaron. Clerics flinch. The world's magic has categorized him: **Neutral Evil.**

Aaron is from a world with no cosmic alignment, so the reading is neither simply true nor false — it's a **measurement of a thing that had no name back home.** What the world's instruments detect is real: his willingness to shade the truth when certain, his transactional coldness, that testimony he lied on. Home called that "a guy who's kind of an asshole." Here it's a metaphysical fact with teeth, and it opens doors (cultists trust him on sight; dark powers find him legible) that his conscience would rather stay shut.

**He does not know about the reading yet.** He'll learn it secondhand — a paladin's hostility, a hermit crossing herself. His first response will be indignation: *"That's an artifact. Recalibrate your instrument."* His arc is discovering that the instrument is fine, and the signal is coming from inside him — and then choosing, over a hundred hours, whether to fight the reading, feed it, or find out if a self-aware Evil is still Evil.

# Arc seeds

1. **The unfinished loop.** The fourteen-second signal is a fragment. The green static recurs, decoding one syllable at a time across the season. It's saying a *name* — possibly the name of whoever crossed over before him.
2. **He is not the first.** Evidence of a prior modern arrival, decades gone: a rusted thing that shouldn't exist, a legend of a "static-man," a survivor.
3. **The reading and the choice.** Cultists at the Caves of Chaos see a friend in him. The Keep's chapel sees a threat. He can walk the line for only so long before the line names him.
4. **Redemption is available and he keeps declining it.** The world literally offers alignment change through act and cost. He knows this. He keeps choosing the efficient path.
5. **CON 18 as a curse.** He's terribly hard to kill. Things that would mercifully end others merely *maim* and continue. What survives when it can't die?
6. **The forty-dollar client.** Someone back home sent that file on purpose. Who, and why him, and are they still transmitting?
7. **The spellbook is growing.** New spells arrive not by study but by *reception* — as if the static is uploading them. This should terrify him more than it does.
8. **The one relationship.** A single NPC he cannot categorize, whom his CHA 9 keeps sabotaging, who represents the WIS he never had.

# Hard canon

- Name is **Aaron Fischer.** Human Magic-User, level 1, Neutral Evil (per the world's reading).
- **6 HP, AC 9, no armor.** Dagger, dart, or staff only. He is physically fragile and knows it.
- **INT 18, CON 18, DEX 15, STR 12, CHA 9, WIS 9.** Brilliant, tireless, coordinated, cold, and prone to catastrophic misjudgment.
- Spells known: **Read Magic, Unseen Servant, Sleep, Comprehend Languages.** One 1st-level slot per day.
- From 21st-century Rochester, NY. Former **audio forensics analyst**, disgraced over shaded testimony.
- The Crossing involved a **green-static audio signal** he amplified. Its origin is unknown and stays unknown.
- He is **lapsed/shallow** on D&D — recognizes it, sneers at it, misjudges it.
- He **does not yet know** he reads as Evil.
- His world has **no native cosmic alignment.**
- Status: **alive.** He does not know why that keeps being true.
