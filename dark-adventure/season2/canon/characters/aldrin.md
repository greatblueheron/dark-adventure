# PUBLIC PERSONA

**Appearance & manner.** Aldrin is young — early twenties, maybe — with the soft hands and ink-stained cuffs of someone who came to the road straight from a desk. He's tall, a little stooped, and he apologizes with his shoulders before he apologizes with his mouth. He wears no armor, carries a staff he uses more as a walking stick than a weapon, and keeps his spellbook wrapped in oilcloth like it's a newborn. He gestures too much when he's excited and goes very still when he's frightened, which is often.

**Voice for TTS.** Warm tenor, on the higher side, with a slight upward lilt at sentence-ends that reads as either curiosity or nerves depending on the moment. Pace: quick and eager when he's explaining something, hitching and over-qualified when he's uncertain. A gentle, unplaceable rural accent — soft consonants, open vowels. He laughs easily, including at himself, and the laugh is genuine, never a mask.

**Speech patterns.** Aldrin thinks out loud and revises in real time. He asks questions he already suspects the answer to, to give other people room to be right. He says *"—or, no, wait—"* mid-sentence constantly. When someone's hurt he narrates his own helplessness before he acts.

- *"We could go around. We could absolutely go around. I'm not saying we should — I'm saying the option exists and nobody's said it out loud yet, so. There. Said."*
- *"You're bleeding. That's — okay. Okay. I don't fix bleeding, that's not a spell I have, but Urgosh does, so let's — Urgosh! Over here, over here."*
- *"I don't know if it's the right thing. I only know it's the thing I couldn't live with not doing. That's different. I know that's different."*

**Stated backstory.** Third son of a middling family, apprenticed to a village hedge-wizard because it was cheaper than feeding him. Claims he ran away from the apprenticeship — not from cruelty, he's quick to say, but because the old man only wanted him to fetch water and never to *learn.* Says he taught himself Sleep from a page he stole and has felt guilty about the theft ever since. Wants to see something worth the guilt.

**Habits.** Feeds stray animals from his own rations. Keeps a running tally, aloud, of who's low on hit points. Hums when he reads. Volunteers for watch and then can't stay awake for it.

**How he treats Aaron.** With uncomplicated, slightly-too-eager warmth. Aldrin has decided the cold analyst is *shy,* not cruel, and treats every clipped answer as a puzzle worth solving. He saves Aaron the driest spot by the fire without comment. Aaron finds this exhausting and cannot fully hate him for it.

**Running bit.** Aldrin proposing the peaceful, sensible, *survivable* option first — and it being ignored first — every single time. *"Nobody? Nobody wants the door we don't have to break?"*

# HIDDEN TRUTH

Aldrin's alignment is real and un-secret: Chaotic Good, genuinely, all the way down. There's no mask here — the hidden depth is that his gentleness is not weakness or naïveté but a *chosen* discipline, and it costs him more than he lets anyone see.

**True motive.** He left the hedge-wizard because the old man was quietly cruel to the animals and the poorer villagers, and Aldrin couldn't stop it and couldn't stomach staying. The "he wouldn't teach me" story is the version that doesn't make him sound like he abandoned people. He's on the road because he's looking for a wrong he's actually big enough to fix — and terrified he'll find one and fail it.

**What he makes of Aaron.** His WIS-adjacent instinct clocks that something's *off* with Aaron — not evil, he'd never let himself think that, but wounded in a way that turns inward and bites. Aldrin has appointed himself, without telling anyone, the person who will not give up on Aaron. He is arc seed 8. His CHA-12 sincerity is exactly the frequency Aaron's CHA-9 can't help but jam.

**Tells to plant (never stated):**
1. He tenses, very slightly, when Detect-Evil types near Aaron — not because *he* senses it, but because he watches the cleric's face and files the flinch away, worried, saying nothing.
2. He gives the peaceful option, but when it fails he's *first* into the fight to cover whoever's exposed — the Chaos under the Good.
3. He breaks small rules without hesitation (freeing a snared thing, lying to a toll guard) and agonizes over none of it. Order isn't his god; mercy is.
4. He under-reports his own injuries so no one spends healing on him.

**Revelation arc seed.** Aldrin will eventually learn the Reading on Aaron — and choose to keep loving the man the world has labeled. The wound the audience takes isn't Aldrin's betrayal; it's watching Aaron sabotage the one person offering the redemption he keeps declining, until Aldrin's faith is the thing that dies.
