# PUBLIC PERSONA

**Appearance.** A woman somewhere past thirty, though the party can't pin it closer — one of those faces that reads younger in candlelight and older when she's thinking. Solid, unhurried. Broad hands with ink worked so deep into the creases it won't scrub out. She wears her hair pulled back plainly and keeps a staff she leans on more than she needs to, which Aaron clocks as a habit, not a weakness. No armor, no ornament. She looks like someone who runs an apothecary and settles the neighbors' disputes.

**Manner.** Calm to the point of being a fixed point. When six people are talking over each other in a bad room, Morven is the one who waits, then says the thing that ends the argument. Not warm, not cold — *level.* She listens like it costs her nothing and gives her everything.

**Voice (for casting).** Low-mid alto, warm in the chest, unhurried. A slight rasp on the long vowels, like a smoker who never smoked. Pace deliberate — she lands the ends of sentences instead of trailing off. A plain regional accent, working-class rounded, no aristocracy in it. She never raises her volume; she lowers *yours.*

**Speech patterns.** She asks the question under your question. She rephrases what you said back to you, slightly truer, and lets you sit with it. Rarely gives orders — gives *observations* that leave you only one sensible move.

- "You don't want to go left. You want to be the kind of person who went left. Those are different problems."
- "Enlarge the door, not the fighter. The fighter's already brave enough to get us killed."
- "I'm not on your side, Fischer. I'm just standing near it. Try not to move."

**Stated backstory.** Claims she was a hedge-wife and record-keeper for a river town — births, debts, boundary lines, the small magics of a place. Says she took to the arcane late, after a licensed conjurer passed through and left a spellbook behind for want of coin. Says she travels now to "settle an account," and declines, pleasantly and completely, to say whose.

**Habits.** Keeps a ledger, actual and mental — who owes what, who lied, who's carrying an injury they haven't mentioned. Eats last. Sleeps light. Touches her spellbook the way some people touch a locket.

**How she treats Aaron.** With unnerving, unearned *ease.* Where the cleric flinches, Morven doesn't. She talks to Aaron like he's a fixed quantity she's already priced — which flatters him and, when he lets himself notice, chills him. She corrects his misjudgments without heat and never says *I told you so.* She is the only one who neither likes him nor is repelled by him, and his CHA 9 has nothing to push against.

**Running bit.** She "reads" people aloud like a fortune-teller doing cold reads, except she's always right, and always about something small and true and slightly too intimate — then waves it off. "Lucky guess." It never is.

# HIDDEN TRUTH

**Real alignment: True Neutral — and she means it as a discipline, not a default.** Morven isn't wishy-washy; she's *committed* to balance the way a zealot is committed to a god. She believes the account she's settling is cosmic bookkeeping: that Good and Evil, order and ruin, are ledger columns, and someone has to make them foot. She will help the party and abandon the party by the same principle, without contradiction in her own mind.

**True motive.** The "account" is real. Something — a power, a pact, a debt she inherited — has tasked her with keeping a scale level, and she suspects the scale runs through *Aaron.* She crossed paths with the party deliberately. She is not sure yet whether her job is to protect the Reading, correct it, or *weigh* it.

**What she makes of Aaron.** She sees the Neutral Evil the world sees — she's the one member who perceives it plainly and is *unbothered,* because to her Evil is just a weight, not a sin. She finds him legible, useful, and dangerous in equal measure, and she's genuinely undecided about which column he belongs in. That undecidedness is the only mercy in her.

**Tells (plant, don't state):**
- She reflexively equalizes — heals the strong, ignores the dying, gives the last ration to whoever *didn't* ask. Aaron notices the pattern before the principle.
- She refers to "the account" and immediately changes the subject.
- She won't kill something already losing, and won't spare something already winning. It looks like whimsy. It's arithmetic.
- Her cold-reads land hardest on whoever's morality has tipped that day.
- When Aaron nearly dies and doesn't, she looks *relieved and disappointed at once,* and covers it fast.

**Arc seed.** The account comes due. Morven learns her task is to *choose* what the scale does with Aaron — and discovers, to her horror, that she's started caring which column he lands in. Her betrayal, if it comes, is a betrayal of her own neutrality, and it will cost her the discipline she built a life on.
