# PUBLIC PERSONA

**Appearance.** Big-boned but not brawny — the frame promises more strength than the STR 9 delivers, and he moves like a man who's learned that. Grey-green skin, a lower jaw that carries two tusks he's had filed blunt and polished so they read less like weapons. Ink-black hair kept short and practical. Wears a mace at his belt and a holy symbol he keeps tucked, not displayed. Chainmail that's seen repair. His hands are careful; he sets things down without noise.

**Manner.** The quiet one who's already three moves ahead. Urgosh listens more than he speaks, and when he speaks it's to close a gap in someone else's reasoning, gently, so they don't feel the correction. He doesn't perform piety. He does the arithmetic of a fight — who's bleeding, who's out of spells, how many breaths until the door gives — and reports it flat.

**Voice for TTS.** Low, unhurried baritone with a slight gravel in the low end, like a cello string that hasn't fully settled. Measured pace, deliberate pauses mid-thought where you can hear him weighing the next word. A faint working-class burr worn smooth by education — the accent of someone who taught himself to sound like the temple that took him in. Warm timbre, cold economy.

**Speech patterns.** Precise vocabulary that undersells itself. Asks the question no one else thought of. Ends hard truths softly.

- "You're counting the ones you can see. Count the breathing instead. There's a fifth."
- "I have three prayers before dark and four of us hurt. Tell me who I'm not saving so I don't have to guess."
- "It isn't cowardice to leave. It's arithmetic. We come back with light and numbers."

**Stated backstory.** Raised in an orc-blooded quarter of a river town, taken in by a hospice-order that healed anyone who bled. Claims he chose the cloth because "someone competent should hold the door for the frightened, and I was competent." Doesn't dwell on the half-orc part. Deflects questions about family with a shrug and a fact about the local geography.

**Habits.** Eats last. Sleeps lightly and takes second watch without being asked. Keeps a small tally-stick, notching the dead — not names, notches. Cleans his mace after every fight, methodically, whether or not it's dirty.

**How he treats Aaron.** With wary respect Aaron finds unnervingly mutual. Urgosh is the one member of the party who talks to Aaron as an intellectual equal — trades deductions, defers to his ear for a room, asks his read on a puzzle. But there's a beat of hesitation before he hands Aaron anything, a fractional flinch when Aaron gets too close, quickly covered by courtesy. He never quite meets Aaron's eyes for the full length of a sentence.

**Running bit.** Urgosh "translating" Aaron's anachronisms into something the party will accept — Aaron mutters *"above my pay grade,"* and Urgosh, without missing a beat, renders it: *"He means it's not his decision to make."* He does this deadpan, and it's never clear if he understands the original or is simply the only one clever enough to fake it.

# HIDDEN TRUTH

**Alignment: Neutral Good — genuine, and load-bearing.** No deception here. Urgosh is exactly the man he presents: he heals, he holds the door, he does the frightening math so others don't have to. The INT 17 makes his goodness a *choice he re-argues daily* rather than a reflex — he's smart enough to know how much easier the cold path is, and he declines it anyway, quietly, without credit. That's the depth, not a secret.

**What he makes of Aaron.** Urgosh's faith gives him something Aaron's instruments can't measure: he *reads Evil,* and Aaron pings. That's the flinch. He hasn't told anyone. Here's the wound — Urgosh *likes* Aaron's mind, respects it, wants to be wrong about the reading, and keeps privately hoping the signal is noise. He's a Good man extending grace to a thing his god has already flagged, and he knows he might be a fool for it.

**Leaks (plant, never explain):**
- His eyes drop or slide whenever Aaron holds a gaze too long — the flinch, uncredited.
- He positions himself half a step between Aaron and the more fragile members in tense moments, as if hedging.
- Once, praying over the wounded, he pauses when his hand passes near Aaron and continues a half-second late.
- He asks Aaron oblique moral questions disguised as puzzles: *"If you were certain — truly certain — would you still check your work?"*
- He notches his tally-stick after fights but never notches for Aaron's kills, and won't say why.

**Revelation seed.** Not betrayal — *disclosure.* The season's pressure will force Urgosh to name the reading aloud, to Aaron's face, as an act of terrible honesty rather than accusation. And then the harder question: having named it, does the cleric help Aaron fight it — or was the grace itself the mistake? Urgosh chooses to stay. It may cost him.
