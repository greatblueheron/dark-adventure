# PUBLIC PERSONA

Brennos is the party's front, in every sense — the one who steps into a doorway first and grins at whatever's on the other side. Big-shouldered, broad through the chest, a face made for reassurance: an easy smile, warm eyes, the kind of open expression that makes a nervous village elder decide to trust the armed strangers. He wears his mail like he was born in it and keeps it polished, though he's terrible at telling *why* something matters and habitually walks into rooms he should have surveyed. Aaron clocks him fast: charming, loud, brave, and about as far-seeing as a torch.

**TTS casting:** Baritone, warm and forward-placed, a little too much air in it — a man who enjoys the sound of his own voice and assumes you do too. Unhurried, confident pace that speeds up when he's excited and never slows when he should. Faint rural-working accent, rounded vowels, the diction of someone who talks to crowds for a living and reads very little.

**Speech patterns:** He addresses everyone by an honorific he's decided fits them, usually wrong. He states plans with total conviction and no contingency. He apologizes cheerfully and immediately after being proven reckless.

> "Leave the talking to me — folk *like* a straight answer and a steady hand, and I'm both."

> "It's a door, Fischer. Doors open. What's the worst it's hiding?"

> "See, now that went *nearly* to plan. That's practically a triumph."

**Stated backstory:** Claims he was captain of a merchant caravan's guard — "the man they trusted with the payroll and the pretty daughters both" — until a betrayal he narrates differently each time cost him the post. Says he keeps his word because a man is only his word. Says it a lot.

**Habits:** Sharpens a blade that's already sharp when nervous. Volunteers for the watch and then talks the whole time. Counts the party aloud before moving — "one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, all present" — a small ritual he never skips.

**How he treats Aaron:** With unearned, undentable friendliness. Calls him "our clever man," defers to him on anything involving thinking, and never once notices the coldness Aaron radiates. Where clerics flinch at Aaron, Brennos claps him on the shoulder. Aaron finds this baffling and, though he'd deny it, restful.

**Running bit:** The head-count. Brennos counts the party before every move, gets the number right, and is visibly, briefly *proud* of the number — as if arithmetic were a small victory. The show can escalate this: the day the count comes up short.

# HIDDEN TRUTH

**Lawful Neutral, and it runs deeper than the charm suggests.** Brennos isn't the free-souled adventurer he plays. He is a man organized entirely around *the vow* — any vow, the specific content almost incidental. He needs a code, an oath, a chain of command, a rule to obey, because his WIS 7 means that without an external structure he cannot tell a good decision from a disastrous one, and some buried part of him knows it. The charisma is real; so is the courage. What's hidden is that both are in service to whatever authority he's currently sworn to — and he will keep a bad oath as faithfully as a good one, because to him the keeping *is* the virtue.

The "betrayal" that ended his caravan post was his own: he followed an order to abandon people, kept his word to the wrong man, and has told the story so many times he no longer remembers he was the villain of it.

**What he makes of Aaron:** He has, without announcing it, quietly decided Aaron is the party's real mind and begun deferring to him as an authority — which means Brennos's loyalty is drifting toward the coldest, most legibly-Evil person present, purely because Aaron *sounds* certain. This should chill the listener before it chills Aaron.

**How it leaks (plant, don't state):**
- The head-count isn't affection — it's a *duty*. When someone breaks formation, Brennos's warmth briefly goes flat and procedural before the smile returns.
- He asks, too casually and too often, "So who's in charge here, exactly?" — and looks genuinely unmoored when no one answers.
- Give him an order phrased with authority and he obeys instantly, even a cruel one, then rationalizes it afterward in that same cheerful voice.
- He recites his own code aloud — "a man is his word" — most insistently right after doing something that betrayed the spirit of it.

**Betrayal seed:** When the party fractures along alignment lines, Brennos won't choose good or evil — he'll choose whoever hands him the clearest, most authoritative-sounding oath. If that's Aaron, or worse, Voldek, the party's bravest man becomes the tempter's most reliable sword, and never once feels he did anything but keep faith.
