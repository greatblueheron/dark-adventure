The last clean sound I made in my own world was a fader coming up.

I remember it because I remember all of them — the little mechanical detents under my thumb, the −52 marking on the channel strip, the room tone of my own apartment at two in the morning, which had a low hum from the dead pierogi place's walk-in that no one had unplugged in a year. Forty cycles a second, roughly. I'd stopped hearing it consciously years ago and never once stopped hearing it. That's the job. You don't listen to the signal. You listen to what's under it.

Under this one was a name.

I didn't know it was a name yet. The file was garbage — a WAV pulled off a handheld recorder somebody'd dropped in a quarry, water damage in the metadata, the usual teenagers and wind and one of them saying *dude* in the tone of a person who has never once been in real danger. Forty dollars an episode to make their ghosts audible. I thought they were idiots. I want that on the record, whatever the record is now. I thought they were idiots and I took their money and I was better at my job drunk than most people are sober, and none of that saved me.

There was structure down there. Not noise. Noise doesn't repeat on a fourteen-second loop with a carrier tone holding steady underneath it, syllables riding the carrier like a voice on a radio band that shouldn't exist. I isolated it. I looped it. I notched out the wind and the *dude* and everything human, and what was left had the shape of language and none of the content, and I did the thing I have done ten thousand times without incident.

I turned it up to hear it clean.

The monitors went green. Not the display. The room. The green poured off the walls the way light doesn't — light falls, this *rose*, it came up off the plaster like water finding its level, and the loop hit its fourteenth second, the one that had never once arrived, all the times I'd played it the loop had reset at thirteen-point-something and started over, and this time it *finished*, and the silence after it was the loudest thing I have ever not-heard. My teeth were conducting the tone. I smelled hot electronics and cut grass and a third thing I still don't have a word for and I've spent my whole life finding words for what things sound like.

Then the floor was wet ferns and the ceiling was sky.

I want to be precise, because precision is the only tool I brought. I did not travel. There was no sensation of travel. There was a room, and then there was not a room, and the not-room had a two-and-a-half-second reverb tail on it and no walls to make one, which is impossible, and I lay in the ferns cataloguing the impossibility of the acoustics because the alternative was screaming, and something a long way off was already doing that for me.

It was not a person. I want that on the record too. I've cleaned up a lot of screams. That one had a wet register in it, down at the bottom, a gargle where the breath should be, and it was answered by another one further out, and the reverb told me: open country, tree line to the east absorbing the high end, a rock face somewhere north throwing a slap-back I could have drawn.

Headphones still around my neck. Cable trailing off into the ferns and connected to nothing. I remember thinking, with the part of my brain that never turns off: *that's a phantom load. The circuit's open. It shouldn't carry anything.*

It was carrying, very faintly, a fourteen-second loop.

---

I'd like to say I did something clever. I sat up in the ferns and I was cold and my mouth tasted like a battery and I established the following, in order, because order is what I have instead of courage:

One. The sun was in the wrong place, or I was, which came to the same thing.

Two. My phone was a black rectangle that would not wake, which I'd known before I tried it and tried it anyway, the way you jiggle a light switch in a house you already know the power's out in.

Three. I was not alone, and had not been for at least a minute, because someone with a very low voice had been standing at my four o'clock over the water-slap saying words I didn't have, in a cadence I did — the flat, even cadence of a man reading you the situation and letting you decide.

I turned around too fast. Six people don't make that much sound when they don't want to. There were more than six. I'll get to that.

The one talking was big, grey-green in the skin, two blunt polished tusks catching the wrong light, chainmail with an old repair at the shoulder that changed how it rang when he shifted his weight. He set a hand down flat, palm out, *stay*, and he did it without a sound, which I clocked, because everything he did was quiet in the specific way of a man who has decided noise is a resource he won't waste.

"—don't understand me," he was saying, low, a cello that hadn't finished tuning. "That's all right. Blink if you—"

And then something in his face did a thing.

I've spent eleven years watching faces on video, frame by frame, looking for the tell — the microexpression that says *I'm lying,* the eighth-of-a-second where the mask slips before the person even knows it's slipped. His face did that. A flinch, gone almost before it arrived, a fractional lean-back at the hips his courtesy caught and covered inside half a second. He kept his voice level. But his eyes finished the sentence somewhere off my left shoulder and did not come back to mine for the rest of it.

I filed it. *Artifact,* I thought. *He's frightened of the country, not of me. Recalibrate.*

I'd file that flinch four more times before I understood it, and each time I'd call it an artifact, and each time the instrument was fine.

I didn't understand a word he said. So I did the second-cleverest thing I've ever done, which was reach for the one tool that had crossed over with me — not the headphones, not the dead phone. The other thing. The thing I'd apparently arrived *knowing,* the way you arrive knowing your own name.

There was a spell in my head.

I'll be clear about how that felt, because it's the closest I can get to the center of all this. It did not feel like memory. It felt like a slot. Like a single charge in a battery I hadn't known I was carrying, and inside the charge, folded, was a shape — four shapes, actually, four discrete things sitting in me with weight, and one of them was *for this.* For not-understanding. I reached for it the way you reach for a word on the tip of your tongue and find, instead of the word, a small terrible certainty that you could pull the meaning out of the air itself if you spent the charge.

So I spent it.

I don't know the gestures. My hands knew them. I felt the slot *empty* — a specific daily emptiness, a drained-battery flatness behind the sternum where a second ago there'd been a live thing — and the half-orc's voice resolved out of noise into signal, mid-sentence, the way a station locks in when you nudge the dial the last hair.

"—hurt, or just lost?" is what he was saying. "Because we've a cleric, but I'm the cleric, and I'd like to know which prayer I'm spending before I spend it."

"Lost," I said. My voice worked. Small mercy. "Very lost."

He went still for a moment. Not the flinch. Something else. "You weren't speaking anything a heartbeat ago," he said. "Now you're speaking mine."

"I fixed the signal," I said.

He looked at me a beat longer than was comfortable and then, deliberately, looked away, and I understood I'd said something that mattered and not which thing.

"Urgosh," he said, which I took to be him. He didn't offer a hand. I noted that he didn't offer a hand.

---

There were nine of them. Ten of us, counting me, which somebody did, out loud, immediately.

"—one, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, and our new friend makes ten." Big man, broad, mail polished to a shine that was frankly tactically insane in this light, a smile aimed at me like a lantern. "All present. Ha! Good number, ten. Round." He said it the way you'd announce a birth. He was *proud* of it. "Brennos. You look like you've had a morning, our clever man — you've the face of a fellow who's been *thinking.*"

He'd decided I was clever inside four seconds of meeting me. I'd decided he was a liability inside two. Charming, loud, brave, and about as far-seeing as the torch he wasn't carrying because it was daylight. I clocked him and I clocked that he'd walked up on me from the exact direction the screaming had been in, which meant either the screaming had stopped or Brennos didn't rank screaming very high on his list of concerns. Both, it turned out. Both were true.

They were mercenaries. That's the flattening of it, and the flattening is mostly right. Ten sellswords and spellcasters bound the same direction along the same bad road for the same plain reason: there was work at a place they kept calling the Keep, and the work paid, and the caves near it were said to be full of things that had coin and no further use for it. Ten strangers who'd fallen in together the way strangers do on a road with only one destination — because a bigger group survives the road, and the road was the point.

I got that much standing in the ferns. I got the rest over the day, walking, because I did the only sane thing available to a man with no map and no money and six ways to die: I attached myself to the people with weapons and I *listened.*

The elf found me before I found her, which I want you to understand cost me something.

I hear rooms. It's the one thing I've never once been wrong about. I'd already mapped the party by sound the way I map everything — Brennos's mail jingling a full second before Brennos, the thief's quick nervous feet at the front, the two other robed ones whispering over a book. I had them placed. I had all of them placed.

And then a voice came from my right, low, dry as a leaf underfoot, from a position where I would have sworn on my professional life there was no one standing.

"You're loud," it said. Not unkind. A weather report. "You walk like the ground owes you money."

I turned. She was already there, and had been, blade-slim, a longsword she wore like a spine, dark hair cut by feel. She didn't repeat herself. She watched me a beat longer than the others did — I was starting to keep a tally of who did that — and then she looked back at the road, done with me, unbothered, having filed *me* the way I file everyone, and I hated that she'd gotten there first.

"Four-in-six she surprises the enemy," the thief said cheerfully, appearing at my elbow, hands working a copper coin across the knuckles, *tick, tick, tick.* "Six-in-six she surprises *us.* Kix. You're the fella who fell out of the air, everyone's saying, is that — no, sorry, none of my business, forget I — " The coin vanished and reappeared. "You're made of paper, you know that? No offense. I'm also paper. We can be paper together, it's nicer than being paper alone."

That was Kix. Half-elf, all hands, a voice that started every sentence confident and finished it apologizing. I'd learn to trust Kix's hands and nothing else Kix said, and that turned out to be exactly the right amount.

And there was the young one.

He'd been circling me all morning the way a dog circles a guest — Aldrin, human, early twenties, ink on his cuffs and a spellbook wrapped in oilcloth like something that needed burping. He kept saving me the flatter ground without saying so. He kept a running tally, out loud, of who among them was hurt and who was tired, and nobody asked him to, and nobody told him to stop.

"You had it wrapped round your neck," he said, meaning the headphones, pointing with his whole hand, gesturing too much. "The — the band, with the two cups. Is it for hearing? It looks like it's for hearing. Is it — no, wait, don't tell me, I'll get it wrong and then you'll think I'm stupid, and I'd rather earn that fair." He laughed at himself. It was a real laugh. It was going to be exhausting to be around and I already knew it.

"It's for hearing," I said.

"*Ha* — I knew it." Delighted. Delighted with *me,* who had given him nothing. "What do you hear, then? Right now. If you don't mind. Only you keep tilting your head like there's something the rest of us are missing."

There was. I hadn't decided to tell them.

I told them.

---

"There's a fifth," I said, "and it's breathing."

We'd stopped at a shoulder of rock where the road climbed. Urgosh had counted the way ahead — he counted the living the way Brennos counted us, some private arithmetic that kept him sane — and he'd said *four* about a stand of scrub down the slope where four bodies had gone very still in a way that scrub does not.

Four crouched shapes. I'd have said four too, by eye.

But the rock we were standing on had a good hard face on it, and the scrub was throwing sound back off it, and I'd been listening to that returned sound for thirty seconds without meaning to, the way you can't not read a sign in your own language. Four bodies. Five breathing patterns. The fifth was slower, deeper, further back, and it had that wet gargle in the low end I'd heard in the screaming at dawn, and it was trying very hard to breathe quietly and failing in a way only I could hear because only I was built to catalogue the failure.

Urgosh looked at me. The flinch, and then past it, and then — and this is the thing about Urgosh, the thing that got under my skin and stayed — he *used* it.

"Count the breathing," he said to the others, flat, "not the ones you can see. He's right. There's a fifth, and it's the big one, and it's ours if we're smart and dead-Brennos if we're Brennos."

"I resent that," said Brennos, already grinning, already loosening his sword, "and I forgive it, because it's *true.*"

The evil elf killed the fifth one.

I'm getting ahead. There was a scramble of noise down the slope — the four broke first, small hunched things, and I'll be honest that I recognized them, the shape of them, from a game I played three nights of in a dorm twelve years ago and sneered at. Goblins. I knew the word. I thought I knew the weight of the word. The others hit the four in a wall of noise and mail, Brennos loud and laughing, the thief low and quick behind him, and I stood on the rock like the tourist I was and did not spend the spell I no longer had.

The fifth thing came up out of the back of the scrub — bigger, the gargling breather, half again the height of the goblins — and it came up *fast,* toward the young one, toward Aldrin, who went very still the way frightened people do, all his gestures gone at once.

I didn't see her move. That's the whole point. That's the entry I keep rereading.

I heard mail — *hers,* I'd learn later she oiled it nightly for exactly this — and then I heard the wet thing stop breathing, not slow, *stop,* a single interrupted exhale and a weight going down into the scrub, and by the time I'd found her in the space my ears swore was empty she was already crouched over the body wiping her blade on its own rag of a shirt.

"Thessaly," Kix supplied, at my elbow, unasked, hands going *tick, tick.* "Don't — she's fine, she's very — don't stand between her and the exit is all, she doesn't like it."

The tall lean elf — Thessaly — looked up from the body. Flat voice, dry as Urgosh was warm. She looked at *me,* specifically, out of everyone, and something in her face didn't do the flinch and didn't do Aldrin's eager warmth and didn't do anything at all except *recognize,* the way you nod at someone across a room who's wearing the same uniform.

"He's done," she said, about the thing at her feet. And then, because it was next on some list only she carried: "Check his boots."

Something happened in my chest when she looked at me like that. I want to be precise even here. Especially here. It was not fear. I've spent my whole life among people who flinch a beat before they know they're flinching, and here was one who didn't, who looked at me and found me *legible,* and to a man with my particular history of being found cold, being found *readable* landed like the first warm thing in a cold place.

I didn't have a category for it. I filed it anyway. I filed it wrong. I'd keep filing it wrong for a long time.

---

The weight came at the top of the climb.

We'd cleared the four and the fifth — I'd had no hand in it, had stood on my rock and *heard* it, which was apparently enough — and we came up over the shoulder onto flat ground with the wind clean across it, and something settled behind my sternum with an actual, physical *heft.* Like a coin dropped into a slot in my chest. Like a package delivered to an address I hadn't given out.

I stopped walking.

It wasn't pain. It was *acquisition.* A small dense weight arriving from nowhere and finding its place low and central, and along with the weight came a knowing, the same uninvited certainty that had handed me the spell in the ferns — a knowing about myself, laid out flat and clean, the way I'd lay out a signal chain before starting work.

And here is where I have to tell you the truth about what I heard, because it did not come to me as words on a page. There was no page. There was no glowing script, whatever a listener wants to imagine. It came as an *inventory,* in my own voice, in my own idiom, the way I'd read out a channel strip to myself at two in the morning with no one to hear it.

*Six,* I said, to no one, on the flat ground with the wind taking it. *Six hit points. That's the whole budget.* A goblin's paring knife, I already suspected, was worth half of it. A bad fall was worth all of it.

*No armor. Nothing between the world and me but the shirt I crossed over in — call it the worst possible number and stop flinching at it.*

*Strong enough. Fast — better than fast, coordinated, the hands know things I don't.* That tracked; that was the spell in the ferns. *Never sick a day, and now I know why the fevers never took — I'm built to survive things that should end me. Built to not-die. Which is not the same as built to live, and I'd want that distinction on the record.*

*Sharp.* The inventory said *sharp* and it was the loudest reading on the whole strip, the one I'd have led with, the one that had been my whole life and my whole prison. *Sharpest thing on this road by a wide margin and it will not save me, because the two readings that come next are the low end, the ones I've spent thirty-four years turning down so I don't have to hear them:*

*Cold. The number that says people find me cold. Fine.*

*And the last one.* The last one sat under everything, the forty-cycle hum I'd stopped consciously hearing years ago and never once stopped hearing. The number for judgment. The number for the difference between being right and being *wise.* The inventory read it out flat, no mercy in it, my own voice: *Low. You are not as wise as you are certain, and you have never once in your life believed that, and it is going to cost you something you can't yet price.*

"Fischer." Urgosh, close, quiet. He'd stopped when I stopped. He was watching me with that half-covered flinch, and now there was a question under it. "You've gone somewhere."

"I'm doing inventory," I said.

"Of what?"

"Myself." I turned it over, this delivered weight, this uninvited spec sheet. Nobody had taught it to me. Nobody had handed me a book. It had simply *arrived,* the way the spell had arrived, the way the loop was still faintly arriving in headphones connected to nothing. New capacity I hadn't earned by study, provisioned into me from somewhere upstream, and the sane response to that is terror.

I felt the terror. I turned it down. That's a thing I know how to do to a signal I don't want in the mix.

I should not have turned it down. I want that on the record too.

"He means," said Urgosh, to the others, without my asking, rendering me into something they'd accept — I hadn't said anything the others heard as strange, but he'd heard it, or heard *around* it — "that he's taking stock. Let him. It's good sense."

He does that, I'd learn. Translates me. It's never clear if he understands the original or is just the only one clever enough to fake it.

---

We came in sight of the Keep at the failing edge of the day, when the light gets long and honest and every surface starts telling you what it's made of.

I'll keep it short, because that's what it was, from that distance — a hard shape on a rise, cut stone gone gold in the low sun, a wall that meant it, a gate that was closed and would open for coin or credentials and nothing else. It sat there the way a fact sits there. Behind it, further off, the country folded down into a dark seam in the hills, and I didn't need anyone to point and say *the caves are down there* because I could hear what the seam was doing to the wind, the way it swallowed the high end and gave nothing back, a hole in the sound of the world.

Brennos counted us again at the foot of the road. "One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine — and our clever man is *ten.*" Proud of it. Ten. Round.

Aldrin drifted up beside me while the others sorted out who'd talk to the gate. He'd been quiet a while, which for Aldrin was an event.

"You keep touching that band round your neck," he said, softly, the eager gone out of him, just the noticing left. "The hearing thing. Is it — does it still work? Where you're from?"

And here is the thing I have not told them, and did not tell him, and am only telling the record now because the record is the only place it's safe.

The headphones are still connected to nothing. I've checked. There is no cable, no jack, no source, no phantom load, no circuit, nothing. By every law I brought with me they are two inert cups of foam and plastic hanging round the neck of a man thirty-four years and one impossible afternoon from home.

And underneath the wind, underneath the seam in the hills swallowing the high end, underneath Brennos's proud arithmetic and the gate-guard's challenge starting up thin and far — under all of it, at the very bottom of the mix, where only I am built to listen —

the loop is still running.

It's *present-tense* now, and I hate that it is, because tense is a thing I control and this I do not. It comes up green at the edges of my hearing the way the room came up green, and I am inside it, live, no distance, and it has *advanced.* At dawn it finished for the first time — the fourteenth second that never used to arrive. Now there's a fifteenth. There's a syllable past the end of the loop that was not there this morning, one clean new increment, and I understand three things about it standing at the foot of the Keep with the sun going and Aldrin waiting for an answer I'm not going to give:

It is a name.

It is not mine.

And it is not finished being spoken.

"It still works," I told Aldrin.

I didn't tell him what it's saying.
