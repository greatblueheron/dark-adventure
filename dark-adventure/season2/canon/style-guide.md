# STYLE GUIDE — *Whispers in Green Static*
*Governing document, all scenes. Seed 896247.*

---

# POV & tense

First person, Aaron Fischer, exclusively. We never leave his skull. If he didn't hear it, smell it, or deduce it, it isn't on the page.

Tense is **past, narrated close** — the incident-report voice. He's writing this down after the fact for a reader he's decided doesn't exist. That retrospective gap is a feature: it lets him foreshadow with contempt (*"I'd learn what six hit points meant about forty seconds later"*) and lets dread pool in the space between what he knew then and what he knows now. Slip to present tense **only** for the green static itself — when it transmits, the report breaks and he's inside it, live, no distance. That tense-break is the show's single most important prose signal. Hoard it. Two, maybe three times a season it should feel like the floor tilting.

# litRPG conventions

Aaron does not see a screen. There is no HUD, no blue box, no floating text. The **System** reaches him as *sensation* — XP as a physical weight settling behind the sternum, a spent spell slot as a specific daily emptiness like a drained battery, a failed save as the felt lurch of almost. He reverse-engineers reality into a spec sheet because that's who he is; the tragedy is that reality is reading the sheet back to him.

**CRITICAL RULE: the prose never invents a number.** Every HP total, damage figure, die result, XP award, and AC comes from the engine's mechanics log and nowhere else. If the log says he took 3, he took 3. The prose's job is to *translate* the log's number into Aaron's perception — but the integer is load-bearing and non-negotiable. When in doubt, don't state a number; describe the sensation and let the log stand behind it. Never round, never dramatize a 4 into "half his blood." He's an analyst; he'd know the difference and so do we.

Level-ups are not fanfare. They arrive as a quiet, disquieting *re-provisioning* — new capacity he didn't earn by study, which frightens him (arc seed 7) even when he pretends it's just data. Spells that arrive by "reception" rather than learning get the static's fingerprint on them.

**No one else perceives the System as a system.** Aldrin thinks magic is magic. To the rest of the party, hit points are just *how alive you feel.* Aaron is alone in treating existence as instrumented — and he must never confirm whether that's because he's right or because the static rewired his perception. Leave it open.

# Audio-first prose rules

This is heard, not read. Write for the ear.

- **No visual-only formatting.** No stat blocks on the page, no asterisks, no ALL-CAPS screens, no em-dash tables. If it can't be spoken by a narrator, it doesn't exist.
- **Voice the numbers.** When mechanics surface, Aaron *says* them, plainly, in his rating cadence: *"Six hit points. A goblin's paring knife is worth three of them."* The System speaks in his idiom — decibels, faders, signal-to-noise — never in game-manual syntax.
- **Rhythm is meaning.** Short sentences under pressure. Let a one-word sentence land like a gunshot's transient. His **"Fine."** is a full stop with a body count.
- **Attribution must carry voice.** In audio, a listener loses track of who's speaking. Tag dialogue generously but never blandly — attribute by *how* a thing is said and by acoustic placement (*from my left, over the water-slap*). Aaron notices where a voice is in the room before he notices what it wants.
- **Acoustics first.** Every new space gets its reverb tail, its materials, its absorption before it gets its threat. That's his lens and the show's signature. A room is a filter; he hears what it's doing to sound before he sees what's in it.
- Sound effects live *in the prose*, described, never scripted. Don't write [CLANG]. Write the strike and its decay.

# Tone

A three-part blend, always in tension: **dread, wonder, gallows humour.** Dread is the load-bearing wall. Wonder he feels and resents feeling — it leaks out sideways, in the precision of his attention, never in an exclamation. Humour is contempt aimed slightly inward: the anachronisms nobody catches (*"unsubscribe," "above my pay grade," "the DMV"*), the threat-ratings, the hoarded swear that detonates because he spends them so rarely.

The show **never** does: earned warmth without visible cost; awe stated out loud; whimsy; quippy banter for its own sake; a narrator who's in on the joke with the audience over the characters' heads in a cruel way. Aaron's cruelty is real and has weight — it should cost the reader something to like him. Don't let the jokes launder the alignment.

# Ensemble handling

Ten characters, one skull. Aaron only knows what he perceives, so NPCs are rendered by **behaviour and sound**, never interiority. Give each a distinct acoustic and verbal fingerprint so a blind listener never confuses them:

- **Urgosh** — half-orc cleric, INT 17, the one who *thinks*; his voice is the one Aaron respects and won't admit it. The cleric flinches at Aaron (the Reading, arc 3) — plant it, don't explain it.
- **Thessaly** — the other Neutral Evil; she reads *legible* to Aaron in a way that unsettles him. Sparse speech (INT 9), sudden violence.
- **Voldek** — Chaotic Evil, silver-tongued (Friends, CHA 14). A tempter. Trusts Aaron too fast.
- **Brennos** (CHA 16, WIS 7) — charismatic, unwise, front-line. **Kaelith** — quiet elf blade, stealthy, economical. **Kix** — half-elf thief, quick, nervous energy, hands always working. **Aldrin** — Chaotic Good, the conscience Aaron sabotages (arc 8 candidate). **Morven / Seraphel** — the other magic-users; differentiate by spell-choice and temperament, don't let the three MUs blur.

Rotate spotlight by scene, but the *camera* never leaves Aaron. Others get depth only as he's forced to perceive it — usually when they surprise him, which his WIS 9 ensures they do.

# Violence & death

This engine kills, permanently, by the dice. Deaths are not narratively earned or foreshadowed for drama — they arrive *as the log dictates*, often stupid, often fast, and the prose honours that randomness. A named party member can die to a single bad roll. When they do: no slow-motion, no last words unless they fit the person, no music-swell. Aaron notices the *acoustics of dying* — the change in someone's breathing, where the body falls, the silence-shape after. His CON 18 makes his own survival obscene: things that would kill others merely maim him and continue (arc 5). Let that horror accrete. He does not grieve cleanly; he catalogues, and hates himself in the catalogue.

# Episode shape

Target **3,200–4,500 words** (25–35 min read-aloud).

- **Cold open**, always — in-scene, mid-tension, no throat-clearing. Preferably an acoustic hook.
- **No recap paragraph.** If context is needed, Aaron supplies it inside the scene, resentfully, in one or two lines.
- **Cliffhanger discipline:** end on a turn, not a stall. Every third or fourth episode may end on the green static breaking through (present-tense) — ration these ruthlessly. Most episodes end on a smaller, harder beat: a number that just got worse, a door that shouldn't have opened, a cleric who won't meet his eye.
- One clean emotional or informational payload per episode. Don't overload.

# Forbidden list

- **No invented numbers.** See CRITICAL RULE. The log is scripture.
- **No fantasy-native slang** unironically — no *m'lord, verily, aye.* If Aaron says them, they're flagged mockery.
- **No** *"I've read this module," "roll for initiative," "NPC," "the DM,"* or any fourth-wall gaming vocabulary. Aaron doesn't know he's in a game. He knows he's in *trouble.*
- **No visual HUD**, no on-page stat blocks, no [SFX] tags.
- **No warmth without cost.** No pet names. No "buddy."
- **No awe spoken aloud.** Wonder leaks; it never announces.
- **No comedy-cartoon swearing.** Hoard the *Christ*, hoard the *shit.*
- **No confirming the Reading is true or false**, no confirming the System is objective or a delusion, no explaining the green static's origin. The three central mysteries stay sealed. Ambiguity is the product.
- **No cruelty laundered by jokes.** The alignment is real. Let it read.
